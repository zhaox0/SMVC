Content-Synchronous Time-Varying Style Modeling for Switchable-Modality Expressive Voice Conversion

Code

Abstract

Expressive voice conversion (VC) aims to transfer speaker and emotional characteristics while preserving linguistic content. However, existing methods often rely on global style representations and a single reference modality, limiting their ability to model time-varying style characteristics and adapt to diverse scenarios. To address these issues, we propose Switchable-Modality Voice Conversion (SMVC), a unified expressive VC framework that supports either a facial image or reference speech as the target reference. SMVC introduces a Content-Synchronous Time-Varying Style (CSTS) module for content-synchronous style modeling, a multimodal style encoder with a normalizing flow for aligning facial and speech style embeddings, and a content processing block that combines style-dependent duration prediction with Perturbation-Level Mixing (PMix) for target style adaptation and source speaker disentanglement. Experiments under both Seen-to-Seen and Unseen-to-Unseen scenarios show that SMVC consistently outperforms the compared face-based VC methods and remains competitive with representative speech-based methods. Modality-switching experiments demonstrate that a single jointly trained model can effectively support both reference modalities, while ablation and further analyses verify the contributions of the proposed components and the effectiveness of style-dependent duration predictor.
